## Interpretation of f1 (negative pole)

### Preferred label
**Situated personal testimony, cultural memory, and human-centred advocacy**

This pole encodes discourse that grounds broader ethical, social, cultural, or professional claims in **particular people, places, institutions, experiences, and historical moments**. High-scoring texts frequently take the form of memoir, case history, professional reminiscence, or testimony. They use individual experience to address adversity, dignity, agency, inclusion, creativity, and institutional injustice.

### Evidence from the loadings

The loading set is heterogeneous and contains many proper names and specialised, relatively rare lexical items. This suggests an **entity-rich and topically particularised pole**, rather than a narrowly defined subject area.

Several overlapping discourse clusters are visible:

- **Politics, conflict, and collective rights:** *McConnell, Brexit, AfD, Narendra, Bannon, Baltics, de-nazification, hawkish, Ukrainians, Palestinians, self-determination, detention*.
- **Identity, inclusion, and social experience:** *biracial, coming-out, inclusion, distancing, healthcare, burnout, exhaustion, unhappiness*.
- **Art, design, media, and material practice:** *filmmakers, studios, Photoshop, selfie, letterform, workmanship, anvil, knead, planar, Alessi, MOCA, Grimshaw, Skidmore, Owings*.
- **Named people, organisations, and places:** *McCann, Erickson, Waverly, Hartford, Rockland, Angkor, Ritz-Carlton, Bloomingdale*. These locate accounts in concrete biographical, institutional, or cultural settings.
- **Evaluation and emotional experience:** *bleakness, self-delusion, untroubled, light-hearted, splendid, sinful, unselfish*. These support reflective and morally evaluative discourse.

The highly loaded political terms are not prominent in the supplied excerpts, but they remain important because the loadings represent the full subcorpus. They indicate that the pole also includes **historically situated public-affairs discourse**, especially where named actors, conflicts, and questions of collective agency are central.

### Evidence from the excerpts

The examples strongly foreground personal experience and particular lives:

- The 2025 L’Oréal account combines **family memoir, advertising history, feminism, and women’s self-worth**. Loading items such as *McCann, Erickson, Waverly, parce,* and *bien* anchor the story in a specific campaign and professional milieu.
- Debbie Millman’s 2025 text presents reflective “love letters” to gardens, New York, travel, and storytelling. *Millman* and *Angkor* support a discourse of **cultural memory, shared humanity, and hopeful reconstruction**.
- The 2001 electroshock account is an extended autobiographical case history of mental illness and recovery. Words such as *bleakness, Hartford, untroubled, exorcise,* and *dregs* connect medical history with vulnerable personal disclosure and eventual redemption.
- Eva Zeisel’s 2001 talk is a professional life history centred on making, craftsmanship, migration, imprisonment, and survival. *Knead* and *Rockland* exemplify the pole’s combination of material specificity and biography.
- John Wooden’s 2001 account uses teaching and coaching memories to articulate an ethical philosophy of effort, character, and success. *Outscore, unselfish,* and *industriousness* are directly tied to this values-oriented professional testimony.
- The 1990 architectural talk is a detailed retrospective of creative practice, clients, materials, buildings, collaborators, successes, and failures. It represents the pole’s **design and professional-memory component**, strongly anticipated by the many design-related loadings.
- The 2020 and 2022 examples extend the pattern into advocacy: asylum and *detention*, sexism in music, emotional openness at work, and client agency in the legal system. These texts tell individual stories in order to criticise institutions and argue for humane reform.

The recurring loading words *us* and *back* reinforce two common movements in the excerpts: constructing collective identification and looking back on earlier experiences. Because both are common and semantically broad, however, they should not determine the interpretation by themselves.

### Years driving the pole

Because this is the negative pole, the most relevant years are those with the lowest mean scores:

- **2025: −1.00**, the strongest mean association, but based on only four texts.
- **2001: −0.25**, based on four texts and strongly represented in the excerpts.
- Among better-populated strata, **2020: 5.07**, **2022: 5.21**, **2018: 5.34**, **2023: 5.37**, and **2021: 5.43** show the closest association with this pole.
- **1990: 4.00** also falls relatively close to the pole, but represents only one talk.

The excerpts therefore suggest two especially visible manifestations: **early archival life histories and professional retrospectives**, particularly in 2001, and **recent testimony-based social advocacy**, particularly from 2020 onward. The exceptionally low 2025 mean supports this interpretation, but its very small sample makes it provisional.

### Diachronic tendency

The year means generally decline from the high values of the early and mid-2000s—often around 10–14—to approximately 5–6 from the late 2010s onward. This indicates a broad diachronic movement towards the negative pole. In later TED discourse, this appears as greater prominence for:

- personal and emotionally explicit testimony;
- named cases and individual life stories;
- identity, inclusion, rights, and institutional accountability;
- culturally or historically situated reflection.

The slight increase in 2024 does not overturn the longer trend. The negative 2025 mean may indicate further strengthening, although the sample is too small for a firm conclusion.

## Other plausible labels

1. **Human-centred narratives of adversity, agency, and institutional critique**  
   Emphasises recovery, discrimination, detention, sexism, mental illness, and efforts to restore personal agency.

2. **Biographical and professional testimony grounded in cultural history**  
   Best captures the memoirs of designers, teachers, advertisers, physicians, and other practitioners, together with the many names, places, and institutional terms in the loadings.

3. **Particularised narratives of identity, creativity, and social responsibility**  
   Highlights the pole’s combination of identity discourse, creative practice, ethical reflection, and concrete named examples.

Of these, **“Situated personal testimony, cultural memory, and human-centred advocacy”** is the most inclusive: it accommodates both the biographical and design-oriented excerpts and the political, rights-related, and identity-related vocabulary found across the full loading set.
